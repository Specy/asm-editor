# Tooltips

Status: decided on 2026-10-26, with the context menu's arrow (12-13), and implemented the same day on `feat/tooltips`. Terms are in [CONTEXT.md](../../CONTEXT.md) (**Tooltip**); the opt-in and the description rule are [ADR 0064](../adr/0064-tooltips-are-opt-in-attachments-that-describe-and-never-name.md).

## What it is

One Tooltip for the whole editor: a card with an arrow pointing at the control it explains, shown after a delay, able to hold a snippet, animated. It answers the request for something better than `title` on controls that are not clear on their own, the **Pixel editor**'s first.

## Decisions

1. **Opt-in through an attachment.** `<button {@attach tooltip('Zoom out', { shortcut: 'Minus' })}>`. The attachment mounts the shared Tooltip component; it adds no element around the trigger, and it passes through a component's spread props like any attachment (9). The element drops its `title`, or the browser's own tooltip would follow a second later. Nothing reads `title` attributes from the DOM.
2. **Accessible, always a description.** The trigger has `aria-describedby` pointing at the Tooltip's content, which stays in the accessibility tree while the card is hidden; the name is the element's own text or `aria-label`, never the Tooltip. The card has `role="tooltip"`, takes no focus and holds nothing that can be clicked (a popover is the component for that). Following WCAG 1.4.13: keyboard focus shows it (`:focus-visible`, not the focus a click gives), blur hides it, Escape closes it without moving focus, the pointer can cross from the element onto the card without it closing, and nothing closes it on a timer.
3. **Delay.** It shows 500 ms after the pointer comes onto the element, a default kept in one constant, and a call can pass its own (`{ delay: 200 }`). Once one Tooltip has shown, the next trigger's shows at once, until the pointer has been off every trigger for 300 ms: the only global state the Tooltips share. Keyboard focus shows it at once. It hides about 100 ms after the pointer leaves, not configurable, which is the time to reach the card. Pressing the element closes it, and it stays closed until the pointer leaves and comes back: the person is using the control.
4. **Touch.** A long press of 500 ms shows it, as Android does for toolbar icons; lifting the finger after it shows does not activate the control, and it stays until the next touch anywhere. A tap works as before and shows nothing. Triggers get `-webkit-touch-callout: none`. A control whose long press already does something passes `{ touch: false }` and keeps its Tooltip for the pointer and keyboard only, such as the Pixel editor's palette swatches, which a long press recolours.
5. **Placement and arrow.** Above the element by default, `{ placement: 'bottom' | 'left' | 'right' }` per call. Without room on its side it flips to the opposite side, then slides along that edge to stay 4 px inside the viewport. The arrow points at the element's centre along that edge whatever the slide, held at least a corner radius from the card's corners, with a 6 px gap plus the arrow between card and element. It is anchored to the element, not the pointer. The page scrolling under it closes it. It is placed by hand, as `Select`'s list is (`place()`), in a manual popover in the top layer: no `@floating-ui/dom`, and no CSS anchor positioning.
6. **Animation.** One, built in and not configurable: in over 120 ms with `cubicOut`, fading, sliding 4 px out of the element and scaling from 0.96 from the arrow's tip; out in 80 ms, fading. Moving between triggers while warm (3) swaps them at once: the old one goes without fading, the new one only fades in. Reduced motion makes the duration 0, keeping one path, as `Select` does. A snippet can animate its own content.
7. **Content.** `tooltip(content, options?)` with `content` a `string` or a `Snippet` (no arguments), drawn in the same card with a width of at most about 18rem. A `shortcut` option takes a key in the shortcut store's form (`Mod+KeyZ`) and draws it through `shortcutLabel()` as a `<kbd>` after the text, ⌘Z on a Mac, and part of the description ("Undo, Ctrl+Z"); it replaces the "(Ctrl+Z)" written into titles, which say Ctrl on a Mac too. Empty content (`undefined`, `null`, `''`) means no Tooltip, so a call site can make one conditional. Content that changes while the card is open changes in place.
8. **Disabled controls.** A disabled control takes a Tooltip as it is, with `disabled`: Chrome 153 sends `pointerenter`/`pointerleave` to a disabled button (no `click`), so the pointer shows it, and a screen reader reads the description, but keyboard-only users cannot bring it up, as with `title` today. No control moves to `aria-disabled` for this.
9. **Shared components.** `Button`, `ButtonLink` and `Switch` spread their rest props onto their element, typed as that element's attributes (`HTMLButtonAttributes` and the like), and `Select` onto its trigger button rather than its list, so `<Button {@attach tooltip(…)}>` works as on a plain element. The `title` props of `Button`, `ButtonLink` and `Switch` are gone: a `title` passed to them reaches the element through the spread, as on a plain one. `Select`'s `title` (a visible label above it), `Input`'s (the same), `MenuLink`'s and `CollapsibleSection`'s (visible headings) are not tooltips and stay.
10. **Scope of the first change.** Every button, link, switch and select whose `title` explains it moves to a Tooltip, its `aria-label` added where it is icon-only: the Pixel editor's toolbar, swatches and status facts; the Hex editor's status bar and find bar; `IconRail`; `ExecutionDock`; `BottomPanel`'s tabs; `FileTabs`' buttons; the Edit history and Debug column buttons; `TopBar` and `ProjectTopBar`; the Screen's buttons; `ProjectCard`'s actions; the icon links of the navbar, footer and main navigation; and every caller of `Button`, `ButtonLink`, `Switch` and `Select` passing a tooltip-like `title`. A `title` that only repeats or completes visible text stays (an Explorer row's path, the Symbols panel's names, the memory view's labels). The hover cards over data (the Hex editor's byte card, `ValueDiffer`, the memory view's) and Monaco's hovers are not Tooltips and stay as they are. From then on a control that needs explaining takes a Tooltip.

11. **Look.** The `ContextMenu`'s family, opaque: a solid `--tertiary` surface with `--tertiary-text`, a 1px border of the text colour at 15%, a 0.4rem radius, a softer version of the menu's shadow and 0.8rem text. The card and its 6px arrow are one outline, drawn by the helper the context menu's arrow uses (13), so they join with no seam. Not translucent with blur as the menu is, since an arrow overlapping a translucent card shows a darker patch, and text over a busy background (the Pixel editor's canvas) reads worse. Not inverted, which every theme, people's own included, would have to be worked out for.

## The context menu's arrow

Asked for in the same session, to follow the Tooltips: a `ContextMenu` about one item points at it, so a right click on a File's row points at that File.

12. **Where it opens.** At the pointer, as context menus everywhere do, moved only by the arrow's height and sideways enough to keep the arrow off the corner, its tip on the click point, which is on the item. Opened from the keyboard, the point stays the item's bottom-left corner plus 16px, so the tip touches the row's edge. It flips above the point near the bottom of the screen and slides left near the right edge as now, the arrow following to stay on the point. A menu about no item in particular (the Explorer's empty space, the root folder's menu) has no arrow; the caller says which. The item is still marked while its menu is open, as the Explorer and the Symbols panel already do, and register values gain the Symbols panel's outline. Arrows on context menus are not the usual practice: desktop menus open at the pointer with no arrow and mark the item instead (Finder's ring, VS Code's outline), and arrows belong to popovers. This keeps the usual position and adds the arrow; hanging the menu below the item's edge was set aside because it moves the menu away from the pointer by however much of the row is below the click.
13. **One outline.** The menu stays translucent (85% `--tertiary`, a 4px blur, as Monaco's menu is), so its background, blur and border are drawn as one shape, a rounded rectangle whose edge includes the arrow: a layer behind the items with `backdrop-filter` and a `clip-path` path recomputed with each placement, and an SVG stroke of the same path for the border. One helper works the path out from the size, the corner radius and where the arrow sits, and the Tooltip's card uses it too (11). A square turned 45° over a translucent card would show a darker diamond where the two overlap, with the border running across the arrow's base; making the menu solid would undo its translucent restyle.

## Facts for the plan

- The theme's CSS variables are set on `ThemeProvider`'s `.theme-root` and on scoped themes (`ScopedTheme`, the Pixel editor's own), not on `:root`, so a card mounted under `document.body` would lose them: it has to sit inside its trigger's theme subtree, as `ContextMenu` does.
- `ContextMenu` and `Select` already use a manual popover placed with `getBoundingClientRect`, `Select` with a Svelte transition and reduced motion: the pattern to follow.
- Before this change, 316 `title=` attributes sat in 93 components; 107 on a `<button>` or `<a>`, 59 of those without an `aria-label`, some icon-only, where `title` is the only name (the footer's GitHub and Donate icons, the navbar's AI Chat link, the logo link).
- The component tests stub `showPopover` already; a Tooltip's transition under jsdom needs the `Element.animate` stub.
- `ContextMenu` is opened from five places, each with a point: the Explorer (a row, or the tree's empty space for the root folder), the Symbols panel (a function row, or its touch menu button), `RegisterFileRows` (a value), the memory view and the Hex editor (a byte, or the selection it is in). The menu's translucent restyle was uncommitted in the tree when this was decided.

## Implementation notes (2026-10-26)

- `E/components/shared/floating/`: `floating.ts` places a card against a box or a point (`placeCard`) and draws its outline with the arrow (`outlinePath`); `CardOutline.svelte` draws that outline as the shadow (masked to the outside, so a translucent fill does not show it), the fill, the border and, for the menu, a `backdrop-filter` layer clipped to the same path; `tooltip.svelte.ts` is the attachment and the shared state (the open card, the warm window); `TooltipCard.svelte` the card.
- Every element with a Tooltip mounts its own card, hidden, in a `display: contents` layer appended to its theme subtree (`.theme-scope` or `.theme-root`, as `Editor.svelte` places Monaco's widgets), and points `aria-describedby` at the card's content. A detached attachment disposes its card a microtask later, so the same element attached again in the same update (new content) keeps its card and changes it in place.
- The card is a manual popover that draws no surface of its own and sets no `display`, which would override the popover's hiding; `overflow: visible` undoes the popover default that would clip the arrow. Its animations use the Web Animations API, so the card can stay in the document while hidden.
- Focus shows a card only when the element matches `:focus-visible` and no pointer pressed in the last 300ms: Chrome counts a click into a text field as keyboard focus. A scroll closes a card only when the scrolled element holds its trigger (or is the document), so a Terminal scrolling its output beside a dock button leaves its card alone.
- The first change moved about 110 titles in some 60 components; `aria-label`s were added where a control's words are hidden in narrow layouts (the dock's buttons, the Hex status bar) as well as on icon-only ones. Kept as `title`: text that repeats or completes what is shown (Explorer and tab paths, Symbols and Edit history rows, the embed page's switches beside their labels), text fields (the address box, the Testcase value forms) and the Screen's canvas, and hover cards over data. `DraggableContainer` takes `closeShortcut`; the Search panel's toggles pass their labels (⌥⌘C, Alt+C) as the `shortcut`, since the store's key form has no ⌥.
- The context menu takes `arrow`, set by the Symbols panel, the register values, the memory view, the Hex editor and the Explorer's rows (folders now marked as files are), not by the Explorer's empty space. Its padding grew by the 1px the border used to take.
